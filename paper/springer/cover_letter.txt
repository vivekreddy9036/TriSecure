Cover Letter — Submission to Journal on Information Security (Springer,
journal 13635; formerly EURASIP Journal on Information Security)

Manuscript title: TRIsecure: Design and Evaluation of a Three-Factor
Post-Quantum Biometric E-Voting Framework on Commodity Edge Hardware

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "TRIsecure: Design and Evaluation
of a Three-Factor Post-Quantum Biometric E-Voting Framework on Commodity
Edge Hardware" for consideration as a Research Article in the Journal on
Information Security.

Why this manuscript fits the journal's scope
----------------------------------------------
Voter authentication in TRIsecure V2 is fundamentally a signal-processing
problem: a face-recognition cosine-similarity score and a presentation-
attack (liveness) classification are noisy, adversarially manipulable
signal-level measurements that must be statistically fused with an
independent physical-credential channel (NFC) to reach a reliable
security decision under a tight latency budget. The manuscript's central
contributions are (i) Bayesian signal-level fusion of biometric evidence,
(ii) ISO/IEC 30107-3 presentation-attack detection via spoof-signal
classification, and (iii) an Adaptive Trust Score engine that reweights
these signal sources under contextual risk. Formal DFA-based protocol
verification, post-quantum cryptography, and end-to-end Merkle
verifiability are layered on top of this signal-level foundation as
complementary system-security mechanisms, rather than being the paper's
primary contribution. We believe this places the work squarely within
the journal's stated scope of "signal processing methods for security
and privacy," "biometrics ... and forensic signal analysis," and
"security and privacy of sensing systems and cyber-physical systems,"
evaluated end-to-end on real edge hardware (Raspberry Pi 4).

Issues relating to journal policies
----------------------------------------------
The manuscript includes a proof-of-concept pilot study (Section 4.10,
N=20 participants, including the authors) involving facial biometric
data captured for authentication testing. Participants gave informed
consent, but no formal institutional ethics board (IRB) review was
sought, consistent with common practice for non-clinical engineering
proof-of-concept pilots of this scale. Raw images were discarded
immediately after each attempt; only encrypted embeddings were retained
for the pilot's duration and results are reported only in anonymised,
aggregate form. Full details are in the manuscript's Declarations
section. We flag this proactively in case the editorial office has
additional requirements for human-subject biometric data of this kind.
